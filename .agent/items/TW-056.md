why: `reef-chemistry.md` §29.4, decided 16 Aug. Two warnings, only two, and
**neither depends on the user's band**:
  - **phosphate below 0.03 ppm** — *it is getting quite low*. A warning, not an
    emergency. **It does not escalate**: the app says the same thing at 0.01 as
    at 0.029.
  - **nitrate above 50 ppm** — worth attention, **not urgent**. Published
    evidence shows nitrate is not acutely toxic, so nothing here may reach the
    urgent tier.
Both figures are already in the tree with their sourcing: 0.03 is the published
band floor (`PARAM_DEFS`, `src/lib/constants.js:41`) and 50 is `SAFE_BOUNDS`'
nitrate ceiling (`src/lib/findings.js:125`, comment *"zero starves corals; high
is ugly, not acute"*). Neither is minted here.
the collision item 10 named, resolved: `SAFE_BOUNDS`' phosphate minimum of
**0.01 is a floor on what may be set as a target** — it governs Setup, not
readings — and a reading landing there adds nothing the 0.03 warning has not
already said. The two figures are not two answers to one question. A test
should pin that a 0.01 reading produces exactly one message and not a second,
louder one.
the suspended husbandry expectation, restored but **not verbatim**:
`tests/legacy-port/husbandry.js:84-94` carries `'nitrate 80 is excessive'`
commented out with its reasoning, asserting `urgentAbout(r, /nitrate/i)`.
Nitrate at 80 must now say something, so the check comes back — and it must not
say something *urgent*, so the predicate changes with it. **Restoring it
unaltered would assert the opposite of §29.4.** Per AGENTS.md rule 4 this is an
inversion with its reasoning, not a deletion, and the suspension comment is
replaced by a §29.4 citation rather than dropped. The adjacent live expectation
`'nitrate 25 is high but not acute'` is unaffected and must still pass.
nitrate gets no low warning: two warnings is the whole list, and `SAFE_BOUNDS`'
nitrate minimum of 0.5 is a target floor by the same rule as phosphate's 0.01.
Near-zero nitrate is **not** silent — the existing nutrient findings (both
nutrients near zero together; high alkalinity on lean nutrients) were never
removed by TW-029 and are what covers it. Do not add one.
the case that will look wrong and is not: a fixed warning may fire on an
**in-band** reading — a keeper with a phosphate band of 0.01-0.05 gets "in
band" on the chip and the low warning in the same view. §29.4 settles that this
is not a contradiction: the chip answers *where is this against the range you
chose*, the warning answers *this figure is low whatever range you chose*, and
the app already behaves this way for the dosed elements against safe bounds.
What it may never do is fold both into one sentence as though they were one
judgement — that is a message-consistency finding if it happens.
in plain terms: two things the app will say no matter what range you have set.
Phosphate under 0.03 is getting quite low and it says so once, at the same
volume, all the way down — low nutrients are the ones that actually hurt
corals. Nitrate over 50 gets a mention rather than an alarm. That second one is
the check that had been switched off after the borrowed rules were removed; it
comes back, at the right volume this time, rather than shouting.
