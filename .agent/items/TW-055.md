id: TW-055
title: Out and clearly out for phosphate and nitrate — two named margins, wording only
status: needs-approval
tags: —
order: 690

why: `reef-chemistry.md` §29.3, decided 16 Aug. §27's family gains two figures:
**phosphate 0.10 ppm past the band edge, nitrate 10 ppm past the band edge**.
Out itself keeps no margin — past the edge by any amount, measured on the last
reading (§26).
scope:
  - two named constants in the pattern §27 set for the dosed elements
    (`ALK_CLEARLY_OUT`, `CA_CLEARLY_OUT`, `MG_CLEARLY_OUT`): bare numbers,
    declared beside their engine's other constants, **derived from nothing** —
    not from `STABILITY_RULES`, not from `KIT_PRECISION`, not from the band
    width. §27's third rule is the one a test must pin: adjusting how fast
    counts as moving must never change how far counts as out.
  - the margin decides an adjective and nothing else (§27's same-day
    amendment). Here that is free: per §29.6 there is no recommendation to
    gate, no dose to change and no constraint to relax. A test should assert
    the margin cannot reach a dose figure, because on these two parameters
    that is trivially true and worth keeping true.
the thing not to re-file, and it will look like a bug: at the suggested bands,
clearly-out-**low** is arithmetically unreachable — phosphate 0.03 - 0.10 =
-0.07 ppm, nitrate 5 - 10 = -5 ppm. That is the same *shape* as the negative
`far-out-low` thresholds TW-029 removed and it is **not** the same fault. §29.3
gives the three reasons: it gates an adjective rather than the only thing said
about a low level; phosphate's low side has its own band-independent voice
(TW-056); and near-zero nitrate already speaks through the nutrient findings
that were never removed. On a wider user band it becomes reachable — a keeper
running 0.20-0.40 is clearly out low at 0.10. Whoever builds this should carry
that reasoning into the code as a comment, or it gets re-derived every audit.
in plain terms: past the edge of your range by any amount, you are out. Once
you are well past it — 0.10 for phosphate, 10 for nitrate — the app says so
more plainly. That is a change of wording only, because these two never get a
dose. And the "well below" version cannot fire on the default ranges, which
looks like the old bug and is not: the app has a separate, firmer thing to say
about low phosphate that does not depend on your range at all.
spec: docs/spec/reef-chemistry.md §29.3, §27 (all three rules), §26
repro: none written. Obvious first artefact: a test that a phosphate reading
0.11 ppm past the edge reads "clearly out" and one 0.09 past it does not, and
that neither changes any figure the app prints.
