title: The notice list gets its order — four tiers, proportional distance, fixed order breaking ties
status: needs-approval
tags: —
order: 920

why: `.agent/gap-report.md` G-28. §20 gives one notice per parameter and §25.1
gives the expanded list, and neither said **in what order**. The app answers with
a `rank` 0–8 (`narrative-engine.js:388-540`) that interleaves findings and dose
states and has no entry anywhere in canon.
the rule, decided 16 August (Stage 5b) — **four tiers, in this order**:
1 **alerts** (`alert-low` / `alert-high`, §13; `reef-chemistry.md` §18 for which
parameters can reach them, §32 for ammonia); 2 **out of range**
(`out-of-band-low` / `out-of-band-high`); 3 **relationship notices** (§25.4 kind
3); 4 **everything else, in the fixed parameter order**.
the fixed parameter order: **alkalinity, calcium, magnesium, salinity, nitrate,
phosphate, potassium, ammonia.**
**within tiers 1 and 2**, rank by **proportional distance** — distance past the
nearer edge of the user's target range, divided by that range's width, furthest
first. **The fixed order breaks ties.**
this is the same key TW-076's headline sorts on, and it carries the same
warning: **it is a ranking key, not a margin.** `reef-chemistry.md` §27 rule 3 is
untouched — this key gates no recommendation, suppresses none, relaxes no
constraint and changes no figure. Do not let a reviewer conflate them and do not
reuse it for anything that decides an action.
**the fixed order is a total order, so the list is always deterministic.** That
is the property being bought. Two parameters equally far out in the same tier do
not reorder between renders.
ammonia is last in the fixed order and is never disadvantaged by it — it only
produces a notice when detectable, and detectable is an alert, so it enters at
tier 1 where tier outranks the fixed order absolutely.
**two things are derived, not stated, and are correctable in one line of §25.1
each** — do not harden them into anything an implementer would have to unpick:
**pH sorts last**, after ammonia (the order names eight parameters, the app has
nine, and pH produces notices under §31 despite having no alert tier); and
**relationship notices order among themselves by the earliest parameter they
name** in the fixed order.
not this item: §20's membership rules. This orders the set, it does not change
what is in it — one notice per parameter, superseded not stacked, is unchanged,
and so is the hidden section below the list.
also not this item: **the headline's tie-break**, `wizard-states.md` §25.6
item 6, still open. The fixed order would break it cleanly and is deliberately
not adopted for it. An implementer may not adopt it either.
