why: `THE-ENGINE-PLAN-v2.md` Stage 6b, and
`docs/journeys/journey-4-notifications.md`, which is Dan's own account of the
fault: a notice saying *"calcium is increasing, consider changing the dose"*
sitting beside one saying a correction is in progress, because notices about one
thing **stack instead of replacing**. Five faults are recorded there; four of
them are one fault seen from different angles.
what it is: `src/lib/notices/notice-model.js`, one module answering what the app
tells you about and in what order, and what stops being told when you hide or
switch something off. Twelve exports, no wording, no component, no state.
  - **identity** — `noticeKey`, one live notice **per parameter** (§20). That
    answers journey 4's open question 1 (*"what counts as one topic?"*) from
    canon rather than from this module. A relationship notice keys on its own id,
    since §25.4 kind 3 belongs to no single parameter.
  - **supersession** — `noticeSignature`, the verdict plus the reading it was
    computed from, so a new verdict supersedes rather than joins (§20) and a
    worse number comes straight back.
  - **hiding** — `isHidden`, `hide`, `unhide`, `unhideAll`. Global by
    construction: one store keyed by identity, with **no surface dimension** for
    a second opinion to live in, which is journey 4 fault 1 removed structurally
    rather than by remembering to check. Supersession clears hidden because a
    stored entry only holds while its signature still matches — **that is
    load-bearing** (§25.1) and it is also why the hidden list stops growing,
    which is journey 4 fault 3.
  - **serious** — `isSerious`, `needsHideConfirmation`. §20's mapping: severity
    `act` or a red §3 tone. **Gates the prompt and never the outcome** — `hide`
    does not consult it, because the confirmation is "a speed bump, not an
    exception".
  - **off** — `isOff`, per parameter, permanent, **including alerts and
    safe-bounds excursions** (§25.1), and an off parameter reaches neither the
    live nor the hidden list.
  - **ordering** — `noticeTier`, `proportionalDistance`, and `buildNoticeList`'s
    total order: four tiers, proportional distance within tiers 1 and 2, the
    fixed parameter order breaking ties, pH last, relationship notices by the
    earliest parameter they name. Deterministic for any input order, which is
    asserted directly.
**reused, and named because the plan asked.** `findingKey`, `findingSignature`
and `findingHidden` (`src/components/DoseExpectation.jsx:130-154`) already
implement the identity half, and journey 4 says so: *"Someone started building
exactly this."* **What is reused is the mechanism** — a key for identity, a
signature for supersession, hidden-only-while-the-signature-matches, and its
guard that a bare-date entry **lapses rather than sticking forever**, which is
canon's resurfacing rule already working. `isHidden`'s body is that function
unchanged. **What is not reused is the key's content**: `findingKey` is
`finding|<id>`, one notice per finding **id**, and §20 requires one per
**parameter** — importing it and keying on `f.id` would give a parameter as many
live notices as rules that fired, which is journey 4 fault 4 exactly. The
layering is not the objection: `narrative-engine.js:1` already imports both from
that component. **6f is where the two collapse back into one.**
what is deliberately not done: **nothing calls it**, no surface changes, no
classifier is deleted, `buildFindings` and `narrative-engine.js` are untouched,
and it mints **no wording** — §25.4 says a notice is the verdict rendered and
§19 gives every sentence to the engine, so a title here would be Stage 6c doing
itself early. A test asserts the module returns no prose of its own.
**the golden fingerprint held at `3a782222dbce41c5`**, unmoved, which is the
plan's own test that this stage did not reach further than intended.
the gaps that produced: **seven**, written up in `.agent/stage-6b-gaps.md` with
options, costs and what would make each wrong — against twenty for Stage 6a,
because §20 and §25.1 answer most of journey 4 outright. **Two can change what a
keeper sees and are returned in the result as `gaps` rather than decided**:
whether a parameter's off switch reaches a relationship notice naming it (C-1),
and which of a verdict and a suspect reading is the one live notice for a
parameter when both apply (C-2, returned as `collisions`). One is worth the
owner's eye — ammonia sorts last among alerts, which §25.1 addresses and calls
correct, and which means a detectable ammonia is named after an alert-low
alkalinity (A-3).
