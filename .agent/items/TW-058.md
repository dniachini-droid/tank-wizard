why: `reef-chemistry.md` §29.5, decided 16 Aug. Two halves of one decision, and
they must land together or the app contradicts itself about which of the two
nutrients may be called "rising".
**half one — phosphate says nothing about direction, on any surface.** TW-029
removed the borrowed findings loops, but the summary's `drift:` claims sit on
the stability layer's own fold-mode rules rather than on those loops, so they
survived and can still print *"Phosphate is climbing, not settling"*
(`src/lib/narrative-engine.js:565-591`). Under §29.5 they may not. Count
language replaces slope language **on every surface, or it has not replaced
it** — the strips, the modal, the summary and anything else that reads a
`pattern` of "trending up"/"trending down" for phosphate.
  - the stability **grade** is not direction language and stays: how steady a
    parameter has been is a different claim from which way it is going, and
    §29.7 ratifies phosphate's 14-day proportional window precisely so that
    grade keeps working.
  - `drift:` for the dosed elements is untouched. Nothing here reaches them.
**half two — nitrate keeps a genuine trend**, at **the same evidence bar as
the dosed elements**: one direction, clearing §5's noise floor. It earns no
easier test for being a nutrient and no harder one either.
the chemistry behind the asymmetry, so it is not read as taste: phosphate binds
to rock and sand and is strongly buffered; nitrate has no buffering mechanism
at all. Randy Holmes-Farley's illustration — add 1 ppm phosphate and 100 ppm
nitrate to a tank, and nitrate rises the full 100 while phosphate rises under
0.1. Dan: *"Nitrate can be notoriously stable, or it can continue to rise."*
**BLOCKED on one line from Dan for half two only, named in §29.5.** Dan's words
are *"three readings, one direction, clearing the noise floor"* **and** *"the
same evidence bar as the dosed elements"*. The dosed elements' bar is
`directional()` (`src/lib/findings.js:142-156`): **four readings — three steps,
two thirds agreeing**. Three readings is two steps. The two halves of the
sentence give different numbers and an implementer needs one. Until it is
answered, §29.5 says nitrate's trend takes the dosed-element bar unchanged —
so half two can ship on `directional()` as it stands if Dan prefers, and half
one is not blocked at all.
in plain terms: the app will stop telling you your phosphate is climbing,
because phosphate bounces and that sentence was never something anyone could
measure. It will still tell you how steady phosphate has been, which is a
different question. Nitrate genuinely can climb — nothing in the tank holds it
steady the way rock and sand hold phosphate — so nitrate keeps "this is
rising", held to the same standard of proof as your alkalinity. The only open
bit is whether that proof needs three tests or four.
