why: `reef-chemistry.md` §29.5, decided 16 Aug. Two halves of one decision, and
they must land together or the app contradicts itself about which of the two
nutrients may be called "rising".
**half one — phosphate says nothing about direction, on any surface.** TW-029
removed the borrowed findings loops, but the summary's `drift:` claims sit on
the stability layer's own fold-mode rules rather than on those loops, so they
survived and can still print *"Phosphate is climbing, not settling"*
(`src/lib/narrative-engine.js:565-591`). Under §29.5 they may not. Count
language replaces slope language **on every surface, or it has not replaced
it** — the strips, the modal, the summary and anything else that reads a
`pattern` of "trending up"/"trending down" for phosphate.
  - the stability **grade** is not direction language and stays: how steady a
    parameter has been is a different claim from which way it is going, and
    §29.7 ratifies phosphate's 14-day proportional window precisely so that
    grade keeps working.
  - `drift:` for the dosed elements is untouched. Nothing here reaches them.
**half two — nitrate keeps a genuine trend**, at **three readings, one
direction, clearing §5's noise floor** — settled 16 Aug, later the same day.
**Explicitly not** `directional()`, the four-reading three-step gate the dosed
elements use (`src/lib/findings.js:142-156`). Do not call it, do not copy it,
and do not widen it to four "for consistency": §29.5 withdraws the phrase this
item was originally filed against.
the chemistry behind the asymmetry, so it is not read as taste: phosphate binds
to rock and sand and is strongly buffered; nitrate has no buffering mechanism
at all. Randy Holmes-Farley's illustration — add 1 ppm phosphate and 100 ppm
nitrate to a tank, and nitrate rises the full 100 while phosphate rises under
0.1. Dan: *"Nitrate can be notoriously stable, or it can continue to rise."*
the bar, precisely, and it is not a statistical gate: **three consecutive
readings, both steps in the same direction, with the movement across the three
clearing §5's noise floor.** Three readings is two steps and both must agree —
the third reading is what turns a signal into a fact. The source is
`journey-1-alkalinity.md` §5: *"One reading is notice, two is a signal, three
is a fact. … The app's evidence gates are about statistics; this is about
patience."*
why the lower count is not a lower standard, because it will be challenged:
`directional()` exists to stop a **regression** claiming a slope through
scatter — it is a test about a fitted line. Nitrate is not being fitted; it is
being watched for a run of three, which is a claim about consecutive readings.
Importing the gate would apply a test built for a different question, which is
the §25 defect this whole section removes. The **noise floor stays in force**,
so what drops is the reading count, not the measurement standard. Fewer than
three readings: refuse and name what is missing, per §13's last row.
this item was filed BLOCKED on which count applied and is now unblocked. Dan,
16 Aug: *"My 'same evidence bar' was loose wording — the practical rule from
journey 1 is what I meant."* **Nothing in this item is open.**
in plain terms: the app will stop telling you your phosphate is climbing,
because phosphate bounces and that sentence was never something anyone could
measure. It will still tell you how steady phosphate has been, which is a
different question. Nitrate genuinely can climb — nothing in the tank holds it
steady the way rock and sand hold phosphate — so nitrate keeps "this is
rising" — three tests in a row moving the same way, by more than your kit can
misread. That is your own rule from the alkalinity journey (one is notice, two
is a signal, three is a fact) rather than the stricter four-test statistical
version, which exists to stop the app drawing a line through scattered
readings. This is not a line; it is a run of three.
