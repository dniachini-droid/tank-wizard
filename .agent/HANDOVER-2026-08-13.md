# PART 2 — Spec decisions made today

## Targets: three layers

- **Layer 1, hard safe bounds.** Where harm actually happens. Alk 7–11 dKH,
  calcium 350–500 ppm, magnesium 1150–1600 ppm. The app refuses to let anyone
  target outside these. Not user-adjustable.
- **Layer 2, the user's target and band.** Reefkeepers legitimately run 7 to 11.
  The user sets both. Everything the app says is relative to their number.
- **Layer 3, starting suggestions.** Shown at first setup, clearly labelled,
  editable, never re-applied.

## Suggested band widths

| Alkalinity | 0.6 dKH total (±0.3) |
| Calcium | 50 ppm total (±25) |
| Magnesium | 150 ppm total (±75) |

Alkalinity tightened from 1.0 because published guidance puts weekly drift
under 0.5 dKH. The response to drift is the BRS **10% dose nudge**, not a
correction — a tighter band with a gentler response beats a wide band whose
only tool is a correction.

Bands are NOT derived from test kit precision. Hobby kits are all roughly as
imprecise as each other; asking users which kit they own would add a question
they'd answer badly.

## Rate limits — ONE rail per element

| Alkalinity | 0.5 dKH / day |
| Calcium | 20 ppm / day |
| Magnesium | 25 ppm / day |

Deliberately one number each, not separate drift and correction rails. BRS
allows 50 ppm/day for large calcium corrections and the Aqua Forest label
allows 50 for magnesium; the conservative figure is used anyway, because every
extra number is another thing that can contradict itself.

**This reverses an earlier decision in the session** — magnesium was briefly set
to 50 from the label, then returned to 25 for consistency with calcium. Backlog
item TW-016 covers making the code match.

## Test cadence

Alkalinity 2 days · Calcium 7 days · Magnesium 21 days.

## Terminology

"Net volume" wins over "water volume". The §2 heading and definition line in
`reef-chemistry.md` are exempt — prose about the concept, not app-facing text.

## Corrections to canon made today

- **Magnesium does not precipitate** with calcium or alkalinity. BRS: it can be
  dosed within five minutes of either. The 4-hour separation applies only
  between calcium and alkalinity. My original spec was wrong.
- Magnesium corrections come from a **separate product**, not the daily
  three-part. BRS sells distinct maintenance and general-adjustment mixes;
  corrections over 100 ppm spread over several days.

# PART 4 — The ten rules, reviewed and settled

All promoted into canon, five of them improved during review.

**B1. Kit noise floors.** Alk 0.1 dKH, calcium 10 ppm, magnesium 30 ppm.
Movement smaller than this is the kit lying, not the tank moving.

**B2. Dose-gap trigger.** 12% for alkalinity, 30% for calcium — how far the
daily dose has to be from the calculated ideal before the app raises it.
**The out-of-band halving is removed** (see B5). 12% both in and out of band.

**B3. Magnesium exempt from dose-gap logic.** A 15% magnesium dose error takes
over 1,000 days to clear the 30 ppm noise floor, so it cannot be inferred from
readings. Managed by level. **PARKED** — Dan does dose magnesium daily and does
adjust it, so it stays in the wizard and needs its own approach.

**B4. Bracketing — improved.** If history shows a dose where the level fell and
one where it rose, the true dose is between them. Purely empirical: no solution
strength involved, which makes it the only check on a wrong Setup strength.

Changes made today:
- Memory scaled per element: **30 days alkalinity, 60 days calcium** (roughly
  two settle windows each), replacing a flat 45
- **Old observations may only widen the bracket, never narrow it.** A stale
  observation must never be able to say "that's too much" — a growing tank
  needs more, and the old 25% consumption filter is circular when a tank has
  been under-dosed for weeks
- **Never veto silently.** If bracketing overrules the arithmetic, say so. If
  the arithmetic wanted more, the user decides — that's the growth case.

**B5. Step cap 25%** per change, relaxed when out of range and worsening.
"Worsening" is now defined by the fixed grading rule below.

**§9.1 grading — FIXED.** Previously anything slower than 0.10 dKH/day graded
"stable", so a tank losing 0.02/day crashed quietly while the app said hold.
Simulated over three years: three dose changes, alkalinity 6.87, below the safe
floor.

New rule: **a level outside its band and moving further out is never graded
stable, whatever the rate** — provided the movement clears the kit noise floor
over the fitted window. Only movement *away* counts; out of band but recovering
is fine.

The dose-gap halving existed only as a patch for this. It comes out.

**B6. Correction pacing.** Gentle 25%, steady 50%, quick 100% of the max rate.

**B7. Stale-reading rule.** No correction is proposed unless there is a reading
dated *after* the last intervention. Without it, corrections stack on a level
nobody has seen — a simulation drove calcium 403 → 498 this way.

**B8. Plan expiry on the calendar**, not on readings. `(expected × 2) + 2` days,
so a 3-day plan survives to day 8. Testing a day late doesn't kill it; a month
of silence does.

**B9. Corrections end in the middle third of the band — improved.**

Not at the edge (normal drift takes it straight back out) and not judged by
"levelling off versus still climbing" (unmeasurable while the tank is being
actively pushed).

What happens next is handled by the ordinary machinery: keeps rising → dose gap
goes negative → app suggests coming down. Holds steady → nothing, because the
correction dose turned out to be the right maintenance dose.

**`correction-done` now exits on two readings inside the band, not one.** Same
position in the branch order, no new state. Rationale: three consecutive 0.2
rises don't just stop — reaching the band mid-climb is passing through, not
arriving. While the state holds, it carries "keep testing every 2 days, it may
still be climbing" in the existing `testOn` field, and consumption does not
re-baseline until it clears.

**B10. The wizard owns the verdict.** Every other surface echoes it or stays
quiet. Canon has the principle; the 25-entry ordered table needs promoting too.

## Magnesium — settled, after one correction mid-discussion

Dosed daily from Aquaforest Component 3+, tuned independently of calcium and
alkalinity. Balling guidance says dose all three equally, and Aquaforest says
to set the base dose from the least-consumed element — but reefkeepers dose
independently in practice, and the app supports that.

**The maintenance dose is never tuned from readings.** Not delayed — exempt. A
15% error takes over 1,000 days to clear the 30 ppm noise floor.

**Corrections are not exempt and behave normally.** An earlier draft imposed a
30-day silence after a magnesium correction. That was wrong, and Dan caught it:
a one-off dose of a known quantity into a known volume is measurable within
hours. Expected +150 ppm, measure, confirm it landed — same as alkalinity. The
30-day figure belongs to the slow dose-tuning signal, not to corrections.

Also settled:
- Level watched continuously; outside band or nearing the edge → warn, always,
  regardless of what was recently dosed
- Corrections come from a separate product. Aquaforest is explicit that
  Component 1+2+3+ is maintenance only; BRS sells distinct maintenance and
  general-adjustment magnesium mixes
- Corrections over 100 ppm spread over several days (BRS)
- Every correction logged as "added X ppm on this date"
- No re-warning while a logged correction is settling — "you corrected 6 days
  ago, give it time", never "you are low, correct again"
- Cadence stays 21 days. Aquaforest recommends weekly; 21 reflects what
  reefkeepers actually do and what magnesium's movement justifies.

## Analysis windows — flat, no extension

Alkalinity 14 days, calcium and magnesium 28. The documented 7→21 and 14→35
extension is dropped: stretching means the app quietly changes its own
arithmetic when data is thin, producing a consumption figure the user cannot
tell came from older, less relevant history. Refuse rather than reach.

## `recovering` and `worsening` — kept and documented

Two live states at `state.js:458-472`, in no previous specification, predating
every commit in the repo. Kept, because 8.2 and climbing toward the band is not
the same situation as 8.2 and falling away from it.

Branch 21 now has three outcomes: `recovering` (blue, "Coming back"),
`worsening` (amber, "Moving away"), `off-target` (grey, not moving).

**The wizard has 17 states across 25 return points**, not 15 across 23.

## Component 3+ strength — noted, not acted on

A retailer listing gives 25 mL per 100 L raising magnesium 0.38 ppm — 0.0152
ppm/mL/100 L standard, 0.0304 doubled. The app uses 0.024, verified against the
label two ways this morning. These don't reconcile.

Dan's call: low stakes. Strength is user-entered, and magnesium's dose isn't
tuned from readings anyway. Flagged in `reef-chemistry-MERGED.md` §13.1, no
action.

## The pairing that must not be broken

The stability grading fix (`reef-chemistry` §11) and the removal of the
dose-gap halving (`reef-chemistry` §7) **must ship in the same change**. The
halving is currently the only thing catching slow declines. Remove it before
grading is fixed and slow crashes get worse, not better.

Recorded in `wizard-states-MERGED.md` §9.3 where an implementer will see it.
