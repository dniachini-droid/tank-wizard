Read out of the running code on 2026-08-12. Every formula and threshold was
traced through the source and the arithmetic checked against the running
engine, not written from memory.

Fourth of four documents:

  dosing-spec.txt       the RULES  - bands, rate limits, what may change
  wizard-spec.txt       the STATES - what the card says, in what order
  calculation-spec.txt  the MATHS  - how a daily dose change is worked out
  correction-spec.txt   THIS ONE   - how a correction is sized and tracked

Five things to watch:

1. A CORRECTION REPLACES THE DAILY DOSE. IT IS NOT ADDED TO IT.
   The figure the app gives you is the whole dose to run while correcting -
   maintenance plus the extra needed to move the level. Section 3 shows the
   arithmetic. If an agent reads `dose` as an addition, every correction
   doubles.

2. FOUR SEPARATE FLAGS EXIST TO STOP A CORRECTION, AND THEY ARE NOT
   INTERCHANGEABLE.
   arrived, passed, stalled and backwards each answer a different question and
   each was added after a specific failure. Section 6 records which failure.
   Collapsing them will reintroduce one of four bugs.

3. TIME CHECKS RUN WITH NO READINGS AT ALL.
   dueNow and overrun are measured on the calendar. This is deliberate: a plan
   left unattended must expire. Before this, a three-day plan still read "on
   its way to 9.0dKH" at day forty, by which point the same dose would have
   carried alkalinity past 13.

4. `measuredSince` USES A STRICTLY-GREATER DATE COMPARISON, AND MUST.
   A plan stores a date stamping at midnight; a reading logged at 20:00 on the
   same day sorts after it. Counting that reading as "since" made every plan
   look measured from the moment it began - 48% of plans over three years were
   killed as stalled having never had a chance to work.

5. THE RETURN DOSE IS RECOMPUTED, NOT REPLAYED.
   Section 7. A plan that runs while demand grows must not hand the keeper back
   to a figure that is now short.

================================================================================
CORRECTIONS  -  HOW THEY ARE SIZED AND TRACKED
================================================================================

Two functions. proposeCorrection works out what a correction would involve.
correctionProgress tracks one that is running.


PART ONE  -  proposeCorrection  (84 lines)
==========================================


1. When nothing is offered at all
---------------------------------

Returns null, with no card shown, if any of these hold:

  no assessment, no current reading, or no usable effectPerMl
  the level is INSIDE the band
  a logged correction happened after the newest reading
  a correction plan started after the newest reading
  a dose change happened after the newest reading

The last three are one rule: nothing is proposed on a reading that predates the
app's own intervention.

    const staleAgainst = (when) => when && readingDate <= when.slice(0, 10)

Without it, corrections stack on unmeasured levels. Over three simulated years
this drove calcium from 403 to 498 ppm: a 120 mL correction delivering 56 ppm
was already in flight, the last reading still said 399, and all three paces
offered another. Plans started two days apart and the level oscillated.


2. The target
-------------

    target = (def.min + def.max) / 2

The MIDDLE of the band, never the nearest edge. Correcting to the edge leaves
the tank one bad week from being out again.

    gap = abs(target - level)
    up  = target > level


3. The dose  -  and it REPLACES, not adds
-----------------------------------------

    maxRate = CORRECTION_MAX_RATE[element]
    perDay  = maxRate * paceFraction
    days    = max(1, ceil(gap / perDay))
    delta   = perDay / effectPerMl
    dose    = round((up ? maintenance + delta : maintenance - delta) * 10) / 10

The returned `dose` is the WHOLE daily figure to run while correcting. It
already contains the maintenance portion. It is not an amount to add.

Worked, verified against the running engine - alkalinity at 7.6, band 8.5-9.5,
target 9.0, gap 1.4 dKH, maintenance 9.0 mL, effectPerMl 0.0692:

  pace     perDay      days   dose      returnDose   extra over maintenance
  -------  ----------  -----  --------  -----------  ----------------------
  gentle   0.125/day     12   10.8 mL       9.0 mL              +1.8 mL
  steady   0.250/day      6   12.6 mL       9.0 mL              +3.6 mL
  quick    0.500/day      3   16.2 mL       9.0 mL              +7.2 mL

Pace fractions are 25%, 50% and 100% of the element's max rate.


4. Lowering a level
-------------------

A dose cannot pull a level down. The fastest it can fall is the rate the tank
consumes it, reached by stopping the dose entirely.

If the arithmetic wants a negative dose:

  IF stopping the dose can do the job in a sensible time:
      returns possible: true, atFloor: true, dose: 0
      days = ceil(gap / (maintenance * effectPerMl))
      with a note that stopping is the fastest it can fall

  IF it cannot - magnesium, where the tank uses a fraction of a ppm a day:
      returns possible: false, with stopDays and an explanation

The atFloor branch exists because returning "not at this pace" for all three
paces left the panel with nothing to offer when the obvious move was sitting
right there.

Verified against the running engine - calcium at 530 ppm, band 400-450, target
425, maintenance 12 mL:

  pace     result
  -------  --------------------------------------------------
  gentle   dose 1.3 mL over 21 days  - a reduced dose suffices
  steady   dose 0 mL over 19 days    - atFloor, stop the dose
  quick    dose 0 mL over 19 days    - atFloor, same answer

Note that steady and quick give the same answer. Once the dose is at zero the
level falls at whatever rate the tank consumes it, and no pace can beat that.
The app says so rather than pretending the paces differ.


5. When the solution is the wrong tool
--------------------------------------

    if (up && dose > max(maintenance * 6, maintenance + 40))
        possible: false

A maintenance solution is formulated to replace daily consumption. Using it for
a large correction can call for a dose nobody can pour - magnesium at 1300 ppm
needed 409 mL/day of an 8 mL solution.

Past that ceiling the app says the product is wrong for the job and points at
dry salt or water changes, rather than quoting an impossible volume.

Verified: magnesium at 1150 ppm on an 8 mL/day maintenance dose is refused at
all three paces, with the explanation naming the target and the volume that
would be required.


PART TWO  -  correctionProgress  (103 lines)
============================================

Takes a plan and the readings since it started, and returns the flags the
wizard branches on.


6. The six flags
----------------

Only readings dated on or after plan.startedAt are considered.

ARRIVED
    const lastTwo = rows.slice(-2)
    arrived = lastTwo.length >= 2 && lastTwo.every(r => inBand(r.value))

    TWO consecutive in-band readings, not one. A single reading is a moment,
    not an endpoint, and dropping back to maintenance on it leaves the tank
    sagging straight out again.

PASSED
    passed = up ? latest.value >= plan.target : latest.value <= plan.target

    ONE reading past the target is enough. Deliberately weaker than arrived,
    and the two answer different questions: passing decides when to stop
    PUSHING, confirmation decides when to say DONE. Waiting for confirmation
    before easing off cost real overshoot - calcium reached its band and kept
    climbing to 702 ppm because its weekly cadence put two confirming readings
    a fortnight away, and alkalinity ran to zero on a downward correction for
    the same reason.

DUENOW
    dueNow = expected != null && days >= expected && !passed

    The plan has run as long as it was estimated to need and has not got there.
    Time to test. Continuing to push blind is how calcium overshot to 515 ppm:
    it entered its band on a Monday, the next test was the following Monday,
    and the elevated dose ran the whole week between.

OVERRUN
    overrun = expected != null && days > expected * 2 + 2

    More than twice the estimate. Something is wrong with the assumptions
    rather than with the tank, and quietly continuing to over-dose is the worst
    outcome available.

STALLED
    measuredSince = rows.filter(r => r.date.slice(0,10) > startDay).length
    stalled = days >= 3 && measuredSince >= 1
              && movedSoFar < STABILITY_RULES[element].noiseFloor

    Three days in, at least one reading SINCE the plan began, and the level has
    not moved beyond the kit's noise.

BACKWARDS
    backwards = days >= 3 && measuredSince >= 1
                && ((up && latest.value < plan.startValue)
                 || (!up && latest.value > plan.startValue))

    Same preconditions, but the level has moved AWAY from the target.

The `measuredSince >= 1` condition on the last two is load-bearing. Without it,
the latest reading IS the one the plan started from, movement is measured from
a reading to itself, and comes out zero every time. Absence of evidence was
being read as evidence of failure: 48% of plans over three years were killed as
stalled having never had a chance to work, and each replacement started from a
worse level.

The comparison is strictly greater than startDay, because a plan stored as a
date stamps at midnight and a reading logged at 20:00 the same day sorts after
it.


7. The return dose is recomputed
--------------------------------

    freshReturn = (maintenanceNow is finite and > 0
                   && (plan.returnDose == null
                       || maintenanceNow < plan.returnDose * 6))
                  ? round(maintenanceNow * 10) / 10
                  : plan.returnDose

The dose recorded when the plan started is what the tank used THEN. A plan that
runs for weeks while demand grows would send the keeper back to a figure 38%
short, and the level falls straight out again.

The stored value survives as a sanity floor: if the fresh figure is absurd -
more than six times the recorded one - the recorded one is the safer answer.

Both are returned: `returnDose` is the fresh figure, `storedReturnDose` the
original.


8. With no readings at all
--------------------------

If nothing has been logged since the plan started, the function does NOT return
early with nothing. It returns the time-based flags:

    days      = elapsed since startedAt
    dueNow    = elapsed >= expectedDays
    overrun   = elapsed > expectedDays * 2 + 2
    arrived, passed, stalled, backwards = false
    remaining, daysLeft = null

An elevated dose has been running the whole time and the clock is the one thing
knowable without a test. Returning early here meant a three-day plan still read
"on its way to 9.0dKH" forty days later - by which point the same dose would
have carried alkalinity past 13, the app cheerfully reporting progress on a
correction that had long since become an overdose.


9. Everything the flags feed
----------------------------

From wizard-spec.txt section 2, in the order the wizard tests them:

    overrun && !passed        ->  correction-stalled  "Taking too long"
    dueNow && !overrun        ->  correction-due      "Test now"
    arrived || passed         ->  correction-done     "Target reached"
    backwards || stalled      ->  correction-stalled  "Not responding"
    otherwise                 ->  correcting-dose     "Correction running"

Order matters. A plan that has both overrun and arrived reports as done, not
stalled, because arrived is tested first among the outcome flags.
