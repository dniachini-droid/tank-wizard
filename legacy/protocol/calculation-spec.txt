This is the third of three documents:

  dosing-spec.txt   the RULES  - bands, rate limits, what may be changed
  wizard-spec.txt   the STATES - what the card says and in what order
  calculation-spec  the MATHS  - how a number becomes a recommendation
  (this one)

Four things to watch:

1. THE THREE ELEMENTS USE DIFFERENT STAGING FRACTIONS, ON PURPOSE.
   They are not a copy-paste drift. Alkalinity is read to 0.01 dKH every two
   days and can be trusted to move fast; magnesium is read to 25 ppm every
   three weeks and cannot. Section 6 gives the numbers side by side. An agent
   who unifies them will make magnesium unstable.

2. MAGNESIUM HAS NO RESCUE PATH, AND THAT IS DELIBERATE.
   Alkalinity and calcium apply the full calculated change when the level is
   out of band. Magnesium never does. Raising a dilute maintenance dose to
   lift a low magnesium produces figures nobody would pour - a drifting tank
   once asked for 72 mL/day. Levels are moved by a correction; this dose only
   ever tracks consumption.

3. FIVE CONSTRAINTS APPLY IN A FIXED ORDER AND THE ORDER MATTERS.
   Staging, then rate ceiling, then plausibility, then bracketing, then the
   step cap. Reordering changes the answer. Section 7 sets it out.

4. THE STEP CAP IS USUALLY THE BINDING CONSTRAINT.
   In testing, the rate ceiling almost never bound - the 25% step cap was
   tighter in every realistic case. If an agent is tuning rate limits to
   change behaviour, they are probably tuning the wrong thing.

================================================================================
HOW THE WIZARD CALCULATES A DOSE CHANGE
================================================================================

Alkalinity, calcium and magnesium. The path from readings to a recommended
millilitre figure, in the order the code executes it.


1. The one equation everything rests on
---------------------------------------

    consumption = whatTheDoseSupplies - whatTheLevelDid

    maintenanceDose = consumption / effectPerMl

In the code:

    out.supplied        = out.currentDose * effectPerMl
    out.consumption     = out.supplied - trendPerDay
    out.maintenanceDose = out.consumption / effectPerMl

Read it in plain terms: if you are adding 0.62 dKH a day and the level is
falling by 0.10, the tank is using 0.72. If the level is rising by 0.10, it is
using 0.52.

Everything after this is deciding how much of the gap between currentDose and
maintenanceDose to actually apply, and whether it is safe to apply any of it.


2. Where effectPerMl comes from
-------------------------------

    effectPerMl = strengthPer100L * (100 / volumeL)

Units: strengthPer100L is the change one millilitre makes in 100 litres, so
scaling by (100 / volumeL) gives the change one millilitre makes in this tank.
On 77 L with a 0.0533 solution that is 0.0692 dKH per mL.

The strength comes from Setup. If history contains enough dose changes and
readings, the app solves it from observation instead and compares - a mismatch
raises "dose strengths not yet confirmed".

    WARNING: a wrong strength in Setup makes every figure below wrong, in
    proportion, and invisibly. There is no independent check. This is the
    single largest correctness risk in the system.


3. Where the trend comes from
-----------------------------

A least-squares fit across readings inside the analysis window:

    alkalinity   7 days, extendable to 21
    calcium      14 days, extendable to 35
    magnesium    14 days, extendable to 35

Readings are excluded if they fall inside a disturbance - a water change or a
logged correction - because those move the level without being consumption.

The fit also produces:

    slopeSE      standard error of the slope
    fittedNow    the fitted value at today, used in preference to the last
                 raw reading wherever the app reasons about where the level
                 actually is
    rmse         used to detect an anomalous reading


4. Before any change is calculated
----------------------------------

The wizard returns "hold" early, without calculating, in all of these:

    fewer than 3 usable readings
    inside the settle window since the last dose change
    the trend is within the kit's noise floor
    the trend is not statistically confirmed (calcium, magnesium)
    the level is out of band but moving TOWARD it
    a correction plan is running and progressing normally
    the arithmetic is implausible - see section 9

The fifth is worth naming. If alkalinity is at 8.1 and rising at 0.1 a day, it
is already on its way back. Changing the dose now works against that, so the
app says so and waits.


5. The raw change
-----------------

    rawChange = maintenanceDose - currentDose
    mag       = abs(rawChange)

Positive means dose more. This is the full correction, before any staging.


6. Staging - how much of the raw change to apply
------------------------------------------------

A large calculated change resting on few readings is not applied at once. The
fractions differ per element and the differences are deliberate.

ALKALINITY  (read to 0.01 dKH, tested every 2 days)

    rescue          apply 100%      out of band and still heading away,
                                    or outside safe bounds
    mag <= 2 mL     apply 100%
    mag <= 4 mL     apply 90%
    larger          apply 70% if urgent, otherwise 55%

CALCIUM  (read to 5-15 ppm, tested weekly)

    rescue          apply 100%      out of band, either direction
    mag <= 1 mL     apply 100%
    mag <= 3 mL     apply 85%
    larger          apply 60% if urgent, otherwise 50%

MAGNESIUM  (read to 25 ppm, tested every 3 weeks)

    NO RESCUE PATH
    mag <= 1 mL     apply 100%
    mag <= 3 mL     apply 80%
    larger          apply 55% if urgent, otherwise 45%

"urgent" means the level is out of band, near an edge, or the trend has reached
the meaningful threshold (alkalinity 0.3 dKH/day x 1.5, calcium 20 ppm/week,
magnesium 30 ppm/week).

Why alkalinity is bolder: it is measured precisely and often, so a mistake is
visible within days. Magnesium is measured coarsely and rarely, so a mistake
persists for weeks - it moves in smaller steps and never rescues.

The alkalinity rescue path exists because simulation showed tanks crashing
while the engine politely corrected 70% at a time. Each held-back step costs
another two days at a level the tank should not be at.


7. The five constraints, in order
---------------------------------

Applied to the staged figure, in this sequence. Order changes the answer.

  1  ROUNDING
     to 0.1 mL, and never below zero

  2  RATE CEILING
     the new dose must not move the level faster than
        alkalinity  0.5 dKH/day
        calcium     20 ppm/day
        magnesium   25 ppm/day
     Implemented as safeDoseBand around maintenanceDose. In practice this
     almost never binds - the step cap below is tighter in realistic cases.

  3  PLAUSIBILITY
     if the figure implies a solution strength that cannot be right, the
     wizard stops and says "check setup" rather than quoting it

  4  BRACKETING
     if history contains a dose where the level fell and one where it rose,
     the answer must sit between them. Observations older than 45 days are
     dropped; any whose implied consumption differs from today's by more than
     25% is discarded.

  5  STEP CAP  -  25% per change
     relaxed only when the tank is outside workable range AND still heading
     the wrong way

Then:

    out.recommendedDose = next
    out.action          = next > currentDose ? "increase"
                        : next < currentDose ? "decrease"
                        : "hold"
    out.staged          = mag > 4      (alkalinity; the threshold differs
                                        per element)


8. Continuing an existing plan
------------------------------

If a staged plan is already running, and its destination still agrees with what
the readings now say, the wizard keeps walking it rather than inventing a new
target on every render:

    planLive = the plan's applied dose matches the current dose
               AND abs(plan.target - maintenanceDose) <= max(1.5, target * 20%)

Without this the target would drift every time a reading was logged, and the
keeper would never arrive anywhere.


9. When the arithmetic is impossible
------------------------------------

If the numbers imply something that cannot be true - a solution strength far
from what Setup says, or a dose that would need to be negative and large - the
wizard sets action = "implausible" and shows "Check setup".

It does not quote the figure. A number produced from a broken input is worse
than no number, because it looks like an answer.


10. Worked example
------------------

Alkalinity, 77 L, solution 0.0533 dKH per mL per 100 L.

    effectPerMl     = 0.0533 * (100 / 77)           = 0.0692 dKH per mL
    currentDose     = 9.0 mL/day
    supplied        = 9.0 * 0.0692                  = 0.623 dKH/day
    trend           = -0.10 dKH/day  (falling)
    consumption     = 0.623 - (-0.10)               = 0.723 dKH/day
    maintenanceDose = 0.723 / 0.0692                = 10.4 mL/day
    rawChange       = 10.4 - 9.0                    = +1.4 mL
    mag             = 1.4, which is <= 2            -> apply 100%
    applied         = +1.4
    next            = 10.4, rounded to 10.4

    step cap: 25% of 9.0 = 2.25, so 1.4 is within it
    rate ceiling: 0.5 dKH/day; this change moves 0.10 - within it

    recommendedDose = 10.4 mL/day, action = "increase"

Same tank, falling faster - 0.25 dKH a day instead of 0.10:

    consumption     = 0.623 + 0.25                  = 0.873 dKH/day
    maintenanceDose = 0.873 / 0.0692                = 12.6 mL/day
    rawChange       = +3.6 mL
    mag             = 3.6, which is 2 < mag <= 4    -> apply 90%
    applied         = +3.2
    next            = 12.2

    step cap: 9.0 * 1.25 = 11.25                    -> CLAMPED
    recommendedDose = 11.3 mL/day, action = "increase"

And a tank whose dose is badly short - 5 mL, falling 0.30 a day:

    maintenanceDose = 9.3 mL/day
    rawChange       = +4.3 mL
    mag             = 4.3, which is > 4             -> apply 55%
    applied         = +2.4
    step cap: 5.0 * 1.25 = 6.25                     -> CLAMPED to 6.3

    recommendedDose = 6.3 mL/day, and the card marks it STAGED - the app
    intends to come back for the rest once this step has been measured.

All three figures above were produced by the running code, not worked by hand.


11. Known faults
----------------

11.1  A slow decline is under-served  (UNRESOLVED)

      Section 4 returns "hold" when the trend is within the stable band, and
      that band stays true for a fall of about 0.02 dKH a day. A slowly
      crashing tank never reaches section 5.

      Simulated over three years with demand growing 30% a year, a keeper
      following every recommendation: three dose changes, and alkalinity
      reached 0.5 dKH. The wizard said "hold" at 6.87 - below the safe floor.

      A dose-gap trigger was tightened out of band as a partial rescue. It is a
      patch. The real fix: a level outside its band and moving further out
      should never be graded stable, whatever the rate. That is a change to
      stability grading, not to this calculation.

11.2  The rate ceiling is nearly inert

      Searched across doses of 2 to 14 mL and falls of 0.05 to 0.8 dKH a day:
      the rate ceiling never bound. The step cap was always tighter. It is kept
      because it is the correct backstop, but tuning it will not change
      behaviour.
