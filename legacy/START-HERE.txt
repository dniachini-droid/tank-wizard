WHAT THIS IS
------------

A reef aquarium dosing app. One React file, built as a single self-contained
HTML page. It reads test results a keeper logs, works out what the tank
consumes, and recommends daily doses and one-off corrections for alkalinity,
calcium and magnesium.

The owner (Dan) supplies all reef chemistry expertise and corrects the model on
it. Claude writes all the code. That division has held throughout and works.

THE FOUR SPECIFICATIONS
-----------------------

All in protocol/, all plain text, all read out of the running code rather than
written from memory:

  dosing-spec.txt        bands, rate limits, what may be changed
  wizard-spec.txt        the 15-state machine and the order it decides in
  calculation-spec.txt   how a daily dose change is worked out
  correction-spec.txt    how a correction is sized and tracked

Each opens with handover notes listing what an agent is most likely to break.
Read those notes before changing anything in that area.

HOW THE WORK HAS BEEN DONE
--------------------------

Two habits account for most of what was found. Keep both.

1. BREAK IT ON PURPOSE AND WATCH.
   A test that passes proves nothing until you have seen it fail. Repeatedly in
   this project a check looked correct and was inert: four suites that could
   not fail, a gate flag nothing read, a fingerprint that depended on the time
   of day. Every one was found by deliberately introducing the fault it was
   supposed to catch.

2. WHEN YOU ADD A RULE, CHECK ITS NEIGHBOURS.
   Twice a guard was written for new code while identical old code went on
   breaking the same rule. Building the guard teaches you what to look for; it
   does not occur to you to look where the guard is not.

Two things to distrust in your own work:

  - Test scenarios that cannot reach the branch they claim to test. Several
    "the check is blind" moments were the probe being wrong, not the check.
  - Clock handling in test harnesses. Three separate bugs came from a harness
    measuring against real "today" while sliding dates into the past.


THE ONE UNRESOLVED FAULT
------------------------

A slowly declining tank is under-served. `band === "stable"` stays true for a
fall of about 0.02 dKH a day, so a tank drifting down enters the hold branch
and the wizard says "hold" while the level crashes.

Simulated over three years with demand growing 30% a year, a keeper following
every recommendation: three dose changes, and alkalinity reached 0.5 dKH. The
wizard said "hold" at 6.87, below the safe floor.

A dose-gap trigger was tightened out of band as a partial rescue. It is a
patch. The real fix: a level outside its band and moving further out should
never be graded stable, whatever the rate. That is a change to stability
GRADING, not another threshold.

See dosing-spec.txt section 9.1 and calculation-spec.txt section 11.1. If this
is fixed properly, the out-of-band halving in the dose-gap trigger should
probably come back out, and both specs need updating together.


WHERE THE TEN-STAGE PLAN GOT TO
-------------------------------

A ten-stage test plan was run against the newest feature - the reading
confirmation window after a dose change. Stages 1 to 9 are done and are
recorded in STAGE5-PLAN.md. Stage 10, a mutation sweep over the six message
branches, is not.

Worth knowing: of roughly ten bugs that plan found, most were in code that
predated the feature it was written to test. Scrutiny of a small area finds
faults in whatever it touches.


THE HONEST STATE OF THE APP
---------------------------

Estimates, not measurements:

  dosing arithmetic          95%   the most-tested part by a wide margin
  husbandry bands            95%   all sourced and enforced
  corrections                85%   heavily reworked, never used on a real tank
  reading confirmation       85%   new, and scrutinised hard
  notifications / findings   80%   least systematically audited
  summary and headline       80%   two bugs found in it recently
  data safety                90%   the crash guard is half-built
  anything that renders      60%   NOTHING has ever been rendered in a test

The last line is the important one. There is no React in the sandbox, so no
component has ever been drawn during testing. The one fault that made the app
completely unusable - a misplaced hook that broke the splash screen - shipped
past 38 passing checks and was found by Dan in about thirty seconds.

The parts that could harm a tank are the best tested. The parts most likely to
annoy someone are the least.
